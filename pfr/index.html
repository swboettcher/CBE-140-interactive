The rate law, volume basis, and numerical method
Three short explorations

1. Flow and conversion. Hold the reactor volume fixed. In the ammonia example, double the inlet N₂ flow. Predict how the outlet conversion and concentration profiles will change, then test your prediction.

2. From a shell to a derivative. Select “Balance a shell.” Move the selected shell from inlet to outlet. Compare the inlet flow, outlet flow, and signed generation term. Why is the accumulation always zero?

3. Adding volume. Select “Build the integral” and show the shell sum. Move toward the outlet. Why do the last conversion increments require more volume as the rate falls? Compare with the first-order example.
